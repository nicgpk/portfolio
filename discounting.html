The Problem

Partners didn't understand how discounts stacked, legacy promotions were confusing, and launching new discounting products meant navigating conflicting stakeholder interests on a page with direct revenue impact. As Senior Product Designer, I owned the end-to-end promotions experience across one of Agoda's highest-revenue surfaces — every change had to be surgically precise.

Design Exploration

Three product surfaces that make stacking understandable: simulate net rate before committing, see per-night promo impact on a booking, and learn how discounts apply in sequence.

Process

1

Benchmarking & alignment

Studied how leading platforms handle discount UX. Ran workshops with 5+ business owners to align on partner impact. Partnered with Agoda research team

2

User testing

Tested discount stacking explanations with 12 partners. Iterated until comprehension hit target.

3

Experimentation

Shipped behind feature flags. Measured handle time, activation rates, and complaint volume.

4

Iterative refinement

Continuous improvement based on support tickets and agent feedback over 1.5 years.

Reflections

Removing > Adding

Deprecating legacy promotions was harder than launching new ones. Sunset planning is design work too.

Trust is currency

Design rationale backed by evidence turned adversaries into allies across conflicting interests.

Persistence compounds

1.5 years of small wins added up to double-digit activation gains.

What I'd do differently

Run a pricing perception study earlier. Some partners didn't trust the stacking logic even when it was correct — we fixed this reactively instead of proactively.
