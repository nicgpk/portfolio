My Role

As Senior Product Designer, I owned the end-to-end promotions experience across one of Agoda's highest-revenue surfaces. I navigated conflicting requirements from multiple senior business owners, designed and user-tested discount stacking models, and led the experimentation framework to safely roll out changes on a page where every pixel had revenue impact.

The Problem

Partners didn't understand how discounts stacked, legacy promotions were confusing, and launching new discounting products meant navigating conflicting stakeholder interests on a page with direct revenue impact. Every change had to be surgically precise.

Process

1

Competitive benchmarking

Studied how Booking.com, Expedia, and Airbnb handle discount visibility and stacking logic.

2

Prioritisation & alignment

Ran workshops with 5+ business owners. Built a shared framework around partner impact.

3

User testing

Tested discount stacking explanations with 12 partners. Iterated until comprehension hit target.

4

Experimentation

Shipped behind feature flags. Measured handle time, activation rates, and complaint volume.

5

Iterative refinement

Continuous improvement based on support tickets and agent feedback.

Solutions

Stacking Transparency

Visual model showing how discounts combine with real-time rate preview.

New Discount Types

Clear setup flows with eligibility rules and performance dashboards.

Legacy Deprecation

Phased sunset of confusing legacy promotions with migration paths.

Reflections

High stakes, high precision. On a revenue surface, there is no 'small change' — every label could cost thousands.

Removing > Adding. Deprecating legacy promotions was harder than launching new ones. Sunset planning is design work too.

Trust is currency. Design rationale backed by evidence turned adversaries into allies across conflicting interests.

Persistence compounds. 1.5 years of small wins added up to double-digit activation gains.
