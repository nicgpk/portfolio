Balancing partner clarity with revenue-sensitive changes

My contribution

Led Discounting 2.0 design, making stacked promotions understandable on one of Agoda’s highest-revenue partner surfaces.

The design choice

Explain stacking at three moments: before a partner enables a promotion, in the nightly price breakdown, and through education about stacking rules.

The evidence

Tested explanations with 12 partners, shipped behind feature flags, and refined using support tickets and agent feedback.

ProblemProcessDesign SolutionResults

The Problem

Partners didn't understand how discounts stacked, legacy promotions were confusing, and launching new discounting products meant navigating conflicting stakeholder interests on a page with direct revenue impact. As Senior Product Designer, I led design for the promotions experience across one of Agoda's highest-revenue surfaces - every change had to be surgically precise.

Process

Benchmarking & alignment

Studied how leading platforms handle discount UX. Ran workshops with 5+ business owners to align on partner impact. Partnered with Agoda research team

User testing

Tested discount stacking explanations with 12 partners. Iterated until comprehension hit target.

Experimentation

Shipped behind feature flags. Measured handle time, activation rates, and complaint volume.

Iterative refinement

Continuous improvement based on support tickets and agent feedback over 1.5 years.

Design Solution

The interactive examples below are portfolio reconstructions. Their sample property details, prices, and performance values illustrate the interface; they are not project outcome evidence.

Three product surfaces that make stacking understandable: simulate net rate before committing, see per-night promo impact on a booking, and learn how discounts apply in sequence.
